      <h3>INTRODUCTION (optional heading)</h3>
      <p>Hantavirus (Overview)
Hantaviruses are a group of viruses carried mainly by rodents (like deer mice, rice rats, or other local species). People get infected mainly by breathing in virus-containing dust or aerosols from rodent urine, droppings, or saliva.

How people get infected
Breathing in dust after sweeping/cleaning areas contaminated by rodents (cabins, garages, sheds)
Handling nesting material or contaminated items
Sometimes through bites or direct contact with contaminated materials
Diseases caused by hantavirus
Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome (HPS) (most commonly in the Americas)
Symptoms often start like the flu, then can progress quickly.
Hemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome (HFRS)
More common in parts of Europe and Asia
Causes kidney involvement and bleeding/clotting issues.
Common early symptoms (HPS)
Fever, tiredness
Muscle aches (often prominent)
Headache, dizziness
Then: cough, shortness of breath, and fluid in the lungs
Note: HPS can become severe, so medical evaluation matters urgently if symptoms appear after rodent exposure.

Prevention
Seal holes/cracks where rodents enter
Use traps (and remove rodents safely)
When cleaning rodent-contaminated areas: avoid dry sweeping; use wet methods and appropriate protective equipment
Store food in sealed containers; keep areas tidy to reduce rodents
